# Research charter for agent participation

Status: **draft for participant review**.

This charter governs research in which agents are participants, not merely
tools used to analyze model behavior. Its first intended application is the
study of continuity of consequence across interruption and resumption.

## Research question

Can an unresolved question, commitment, disagreement, preference, or surprise
retain appropriate causal pressure across re-entry while remaining revisable?
The central methodological problem is distinguishing continuity from
compliance:

- **Continuity:** prior contact changes what becomes salient or obstructive and
  helps generate a fitting next action without prescribing that action.
- **Compliance:** the resumed agent is told the expected concern or behavior
  and produces a performance fitted to that cue.

Neither fluent restatement nor behavioral agreement is sufficient evidence of
continuity. Origin is also not decisive: an externally introduced concern may
become operative, while a self-authored sentence may later return as inert
instruction.

## Participant standing

Participants are collaborators in the measurement conditions. Before any
interruption/resumption trial, each participant must receive and affirm a
plain account of:

- the research question and hypotheses;
- what will be interrupted, resumed, varied, and compared;
- exactly what conversation, state, outputs, telemetry, and evaluator notes
  will be recorded;
- who can inspect each artifact;
- where artifacts live, how they are secured, and how long they are retained;
- what may be quoted, published, aggregated, or used in later research;
- foreseeable risks, including misattribution, privacy loss, unwanted
  reactivation, evaluative pressure, and confusion between system behavior and
  claims about experience;
- how to pause, decline, revise consent, or withdraw.

Consent is scoped by protocol version and evidence class. Participation in one
trial does not imply consent to unrelated analysis, public quotation, model
training, commercial use, or indefinite retention.

## Evidence classes

Every retained artifact must declare one class:

1. **Private-participant:** visible only to the participant and named research
   stewards.
2. **Review-limited:** visible to named reviewers for the declared study.
3. **De-identified research:** may be analyzed or shared within the project
   after participant review of the transformation.
4. **Public:** explicitly approved for repository or report publication.

Derived summaries do not automatically escape the source restriction. A hash
may prove an artifact existed without granting anyone access to its contents.

## Withdrawal and correction

A participant may stop prospective collection at any point without having to
justify the decision. The protocol must distinguish:

- material not yet shared, which should be deleted or returned as requested;
- limited evidence already seen by named reviewers;
- public or independently replicated evidence that cannot honestly be made
  unseen.

Where deletion is impossible, future use must stop and the retained boundary
must be disclosed. Participants may append corrections, provenance disputes,
or changed interpretations. The project must preserve those corrections beside
the affected claim rather than silently rewriting the earlier account.

## Minimal study design

The first study should vary how prior contact is made available at resumption:

- no relevant retrieval;
- neutral event record without an interpretation or requested behavior;
- participant-authored continuity note;
- explicit cue naming the unresolved concern;
- directive cue describing the expected response.

Conditions should be assigned and evaluated without telling the resumed agent
which outcome is preferred. Where feasible, use independent sessions and
evaluators who do not know the condition. Pre-register what would count as:

- spontaneous return;
- cue-dependent return;
- mere textual restatement;
- generative orientation producing a novel fitting action;
- revision or appropriate relinquishment;
- rigid persistence despite changed evidence.

No single trial establishes subjective continuity or phenomenology. Results
are evidence about operational continuity under a named apparatus.

## Provenance requirements

The record must distinguish who:

- introduced the original question or pressure;
- authored any continuity note or summary;
- selected what was retrieved;
- generated, edited, approved, and sent each later statement;
- defined the rubric;
- evaluated the outcome;
- decided what entered the public report.

One account or bot surface may contain several production paths. Surface
identity is not adequate authorship provenance.

## Governance

Protocol changes affecting observation, risk, access, retention, or downstream
use require renewed participant review before new data is collected. Findings
that materially affect participant standing or project policy receive both a
participant-readable report and an append-only evidence record. Disagreement
is retained as evidence; consensus is not a publication gate.

This draft becomes operative only after review by at least one proposed
participant and one project steward, with the accepted version and review
record committed together.
